## What happened

East Polynesia is a scatter of fifteen island groups across the eastern Pacific, including Hawaii, Rapa Nui (Easter Island) and Aotearoa (New Zealand). They were the last habitable places on Earth that people reached. The voyagers came from western Polynesia, around Samoa, which had been settled long before. They reached the Society Islands, around Tahiti, in about the 11th century. Between seventy and a few hundred years later they spread in a single burst to almost every remaining island, thousands of kilometres apart, mostly between about 1190 and 1290. The speed of that spread helps explain why people across the region share such closely related languages, cultures and biology.

## How we know

Two independent lines of evidence now agree on the broad picture. The first is radiocarbon dating. In 2011, Janet Wilmshurst and colleagues reviewed 1,434 radiocarbon dates from across East Polynesia and kept only the most reliable: those from short-lived material, which records a single season of growth rather than the long life of a tree. Those dates put the first arrivals in the Society Islands at about 1025 to 1120, and the spread to the rest of East Polynesia some 70 to 265 years later.

The second is genetics. In 2021, Alexander Ioannidis and colleagues compared the genomes of 430 living people from 21 Pacific island populations. Each new island was founded by a small group from the last one, so some genetic variety was lost at every step. Following that loss, they traced a path from Samoa through Rarotonga in the Cook Islands, to the Society Islands in the 11th century, the Austral Islands and Tuamotus in the 12th, and finally the Marquesas, Raivavae and Rapa Nui, which was reached around 1200 by way of Mangareva. The islands where people raised large stone statues turn out to be closely related.

## What's debated

For decades, many researchers argued that East Polynesia was settled much earlier, with some chronologies differing by more than a thousand years. Wilmshurst and colleagues argue that those older dates came from samples with large built-in errors. Even so, as the 2021 genetic study notes, different archaeological surveys still suggest dates several centuries apart, and the order in which islands were reached was long unknown.

A second question was whether Polynesians and Native Americans ever met. The sweet potato and the bottle gourd, both American plants, were growing in Polynesia before Europeans arrived. In 2020, a genetic study of 807 people from Polynesia and the Pacific coast of the Americas found signs of a single contact around 1200, with a group most closely related to Indigenous people of present-day Colombia. The study placed the contact in eastern Polynesia, before Rapa Nui was settled. Which side made the voyage is not known.

## Why it matters

The settlement of East Polynesia completed the human spread across the habitable Earth, apart from Antarctica. It was done by voyaging across an ocean that spans a third of the planet, and it left a family of island societies linked by ancestry and language from Hawaii to Aotearoa.
