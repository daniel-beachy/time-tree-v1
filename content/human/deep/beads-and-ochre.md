## What happened

Long before cave paintings in Europe, people in Africa were making objects that seem to have had no practical use. They pierced small sea-snail shells and wore them as beads. They ground red ochre, an iron-rich earth, into powder and paste. They scratched patterns into lumps of ochre and onto stone. The oldest known beads come from Bizmoune Cave near Essaouira in Morocco, in layers at least 142,000 years old. Similar beads, made from the same kind of shell, appear at Taforalt in eastern Morocco about 82,000 years ago. At Blombos Cave, on the south coast of South Africa, the finds span from about 100,000 to 73,000 years ago.

## How we know

The beads are shells of tiny sea snails, carried to the caves from the shore. Microscope studies of the Blombos shells show that the holes were punched through the shell wall, not made by predators or wear on the beach, and that wear around the holes came from being strung and worn. Several groups of beads were found together, each matching in colour and wear, as if from the same necklace or garment. Some Taforalt shells were covered in red ochre. At Blombos, excavators also found two abalone shells that had held a liquid mixture rich in ochre, lying with bone, charcoal, grindstones and hammerstones used to make it. Thirteen pieces of ochre from the same cave carry deliberately engraved lines, and a flake of stone from the 73,000-year-old layer has crossed lines drawn on it with an ochre crayon.

## What's debated

Archaeologists often treat beads and patterns as signs of symbolic thinking, the ability to let one thing stand for another, such as a person's group or status. The researchers at Bizmoune suggest the beads were signals of identity. But what the objects meant to the people who made them cannot be recovered, and the use of the Blombos ochre paste is unknown: decoration and protecting the skin are both possible. There is also an older argument about timing. Some researchers once linked "modern" behaviour to a burst of change after about 50,000 years ago, when such objects become common in Europe and elsewhere. The African finds show that beads and engraving appeared much earlier.

## Why it matters

Beads are among the earliest objects made to be seen by others. Wearing them only makes sense among people who notice and read such signals. The finds from Morocco and South Africa, thousands of kilometres apart, show that habits like these were part of life for Homo sapiens in Africa tens of thousands of years before similar objects appear in Europe.
