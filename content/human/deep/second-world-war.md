## What happened

Adolf Hitler and the Nazi Party came to power in Germany in January 1933. Hatred of Jews was at the root of their beliefs, and they began to persecute Germany's Jews at once.

Germany invaded Poland on 1 September 1939, and Britain and France declared war two days later. By 1942 Germany and its allies controlled most of Europe. In June 1941 they invaded the Soviet Union. German units began mass shootings of Jewish men, and soon of whole communities, on the edges of towns across eastern Europe. As many as two million Jews were murdered in shootings or in vans that used poison gas.

From late 1941 Nazi Germany built five killing centres in occupied Poland for the sole purpose of murdering Jews with poison gas: Chełmno, Belzec, Sobibor, Treblinka and Auschwitz-Birkenau. Jews from across Europe were deported to them by train. About 2.7 million Jews were murdered there, about a million of them at Auschwitz.

At Stalingrad a German army surrendered to Soviet forces in February 1943, and from then on Germany was pushed back. It surrendered in May 1945. Japan surrendered on 2 September 1945, after atomic bombs destroyed Hiroshima and Nagasaki.

## How we know

As they began to lose, the Nazis tried to destroy the evidence, digging up mass graves and burning records. They did not succeed. German documents survived in great numbers and were used in the trials of Nazi leaders after the war, alongside the accounts of survivors and witnesses.

The figure of six million comes from two kinds of evidence: German records of deportations and killings, and comparisons of census counts of Jewish communities before and after the war. Yad Vashem, Israel's Holocaust memorial, has gathered the names of more than 4.7 million of the victims.

## What's debated

The number cannot be exact. Serious studies give totals between five and six million: early calculations ranged from 5.1 million to 5.95 million, and later ones from 5.29 million to six million.

The Nazis and their allies also murdered millions of other people, among them around 3.3 million Soviet prisoners of war, around 1.8 million non-Jewish Poles, 250,000 to 500,000 Roma, and 250,000 to 300,000 people with disabilities.

Totals for all deaths in the war are rougher still. The museums cited here give 55 million and over 65 million.

## Why it matters

No war has killed more people, and most of the dead were civilians. The Holocaust was an attempt by a modern state to murder an entire people, carried out with railways, record-keeping and purpose-built killing centres.

What came next was shaped by it. The United Nations was formed in 1945 to prevent another such war, and political and military leaders of the defeated powers were put on trial for war crimes.
