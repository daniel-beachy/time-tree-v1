## What happened

In 1831 Charles Darwin, who had been studying theology at Cambridge, joined HMS *Beagle* as naturalist for a five-year voyage. He came home in 1836 with a great collection of specimens, among them finches from the Galápagos Islands, off the coast of Ecuador. Some had stout beaks for seeds and others were suited to insects, and he came to see them as descendants of a single ancestor that had adapted to the foods of different islands.

His explanation was natural selection. Individuals with the characteristics best suited to their surroundings are more likely to survive and reproduce, and they pass those characteristics on. Over time the features become more common, so species change.

Darwin spent many years gathering evidence and writing what he called his "big book". Then, on 18 June 1858, a letter arrived from a younger naturalist, Alfred Russel Wallace, who had reached similar ideas. On the advice of friends the two made a joint announcement, presented at the Linnean Society in London, and their joint paper was published that August. Darwin then wrote a shorter account, which he went on calling an "abstract" until March 1859. The publisher John Murray accepted it in April, and *On the Origin of Species* appeared on 24 November 1859.

## How we know

Darwin's diary and letters survive, as do his publisher's records, so the book's making can be followed in detail. It has a single folded diagram, showing Darwin's view of how species might descend from one another, which was kept in every later edition.

## What's debated

It is often said that all 1,250 copies sold to the public on the first day. Darwin wrote as much in his diary. The bibliographer R. B. Freeman showed that this is a misreading: booksellers ordered about 1,500 copies at the publisher's trade sale two days earlier, and only about 1,170 were available to them. Nobody could know how quickly shops then sold them.

The reception was mixed. The book provoked outrage from some members of the Church of England, because it implicitly contradicted the belief in divine creation, and Darwin was accused of blasphemy. Critics also complained that he had ignored earlier writers on evolution, so he added a historical sketch to the third edition.

## Why it matters

A second printing of 3,000 copies followed within weeks, the largest of any edition in Darwin's lifetime, and he worked with translators to spread the book abroad. Evolution by natural selection is accepted by scientists today as the best explanation, based on evidence, for the diversity of life on Earth. It is the idea behind the other half of this app, the Tree of Life.
