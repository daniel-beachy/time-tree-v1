## What happened

For most of human existence, infectious diseases were the leading causes of illness and death. In 1910 the German scientist Paul Ehrlich brought out Salvarsan, a drug for syphilis found by testing hundreds of chemicals one after another. In the 1930s came the sulfa drugs. On 3 September 1928 Alexander Fleming had noticed that a mould, *Penicillium*, killed bacteria, but he could not purify its active substance. In 1940 a team at Oxford led by Howard Florey and Ernst Chain did, and by 1945 penicillin was being mass-produced. Most kinds of antibiotic in use today were discovered between the 1950s and the 1970s.

Vaccines reached far more people too. Smallpox had killed up to 30 percent of those who caught it, for at least 3,000 years. In 1967 the World Health Organization began an intensified campaign of vaccination and of tracking down cases. The last natural case was in Somalia in 1977, and in 1980 the WHO declared the disease eradicated. In 1974 it had launched a programme to bring vaccines against other diseases to children everywhere.

## How we know

The chart's figures come from United Nations estimates built on censuses, death registers and surveys. The effect of vaccines is harder to see, because it is a count of deaths that did not happen. A 2024 study used models of 14 diseases to compare the real world with one where no one had been vaccinated since 1974. It estimated that vaccination averted 154 million deaths in fifty years, 146 million of them among children under five.

## What's debated

How much of the gain is owed to medicine is a real question. The same study credits vaccination with 40 percent of the fall in infant deaths since 1974, which leaves most of the fall to other causes. The United Nations lists public health, nutrition and personal hygiene alongside medicine. Because the figure for deaths averted comes from models, it depends on their assumptions.

Antibiotics carry a risk of their own. Bacteria evolve resistance to them, as Fleming himself warned, and no new class of antibiotic has been discovered since the 1970s.

## Why it matters

Smallpox is the only human infectious disease ever wiped out. Since 1974 children's chances of surviving have improved in every region of the world, and that is a large part of what lifted the world's average length of life from 46 years in 1950 to 73.
