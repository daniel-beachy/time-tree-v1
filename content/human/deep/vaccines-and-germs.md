## What happened

Smallpox was one of the deadliest diseases in history. In 18th-century Europe it killed about 400,000 people a year, and a third of those who survived went blind. People had long practised variolation: infecting a healthy person on purpose with matter from a smallpox sore, so that they caught a milder case and were protected afterwards. The method reached Europe from Istanbul early in the 1700s. In England it was championed by Lady Mary Wortley Montagu, who had her son inoculated in 1718. It was risky: 2 to 3 percent of variolated people died.

In May 1796 Edward Jenner took matter from the cowpox sores on the hand of a dairymaid, Sarah Nelms, and inoculated an 8-year-old boy, James Phipps. In July he inoculated the boy again, this time with matter from a fresh smallpox sore. No disease developed. Jenner published his cases in 1798 and called the method vaccination, from *vacca*, Latin for cow.

## How we know

Jenner set out his experiment in a booklet, *An Inquiry into the Causes and Effects of the Variolae Vaccinae*, after the Royal Society rejected a short report on it in 1797.

Half a century later, most doctors still believed that diseases spread through "bad air", the miasma theory. During a cholera outbreak around Broad Street in London in 1854, John Snow argued that cholera spread through water. He compared the water sources of people who fell ill and found a clear link with one public pump, and its handle was removed. In 1876 Robert Koch identified the bacterium that causes anthrax, which launched medical bacteriology and a "golden age" of discoveries.

## What's debated

Jenner did not start from nothing. For years he had heard that dairymaids who caught cowpox were protected from smallpox. Parts of his explanation were wrong: his idea that cowpox began as a disease of horses was discredited in his own lifetime. His *Inquiry* met a mixed reaction from doctors, and when he went to London to find volunteers, he found none for three months.

## Why it matters

Later science, including the germ theory of disease and the study of viruses, largely bore out Jenner's main conclusions. Vaccination made it possible to wipe out smallpox altogether. On 8 May 1980 the World Health Assembly announced that the world was free of it.
